Even StrictMath is not frozen: JDK 27 changed pow's bits, and it was right to

In short. java.lang.StrictMath is the part of Java that promises the same bits everywhere, and Spark relies on it for power, exp, ln and their relatives. JDK 27 changed what StrictMath.pow returns on some inputs, on purpose: the old results there were up to 653 ulps off. The same Spark query now gives another double on JDK 27 than on JDK 25. The change is rare, and it is a fix, but "the same bits as StrictMath" turns out to be a promise per JDK.

One query, two JDKs

This is Apache Spark 4.3.0, the same query on the same laptop, once with JAVA_HOME at JDK 25 and once at JDK 27:

SELECT power(1.0000000005093685D, -7.760027956194805E9D);
Spark 4.3.0 on result
JDK 25.0.5.1 0.01920251216909316
JDK 27.0.1.1 0.019202512169093165

Spark's power calls StrictMath.pow, so the result follows the JVM it runs on. The two results are one ulp apart, and the new one is the correctly rounded one: the exact value is 0.01920251216909316303…, 0.57 ulp from JDK 25's result and 0.43 ulp from JDK 27's.

The reason is one sentence of StrictMath's Javadoc. JDK 25's reads:

The Java math library is defined with respect to fdlibm version 5.3.

JDK 27's reads:

The Java math library is defined with respect to fdlibm version 5.3 with a fix to pow so that its error bounds conform to the quality of implementation criteria for pow.

The fix was real

The change is JDK-8326547, "StrictMath.pow returns results outside of a 1 ulp error bound", integrated into the JDK's main line on 21 May 2026 (openjdk/jdk#31210), and so in JDK 27. Every result it changed in my scans lies in one branch of fdlibm's pow (Sun's e_pow.c): |y| above 231 with |x| within about 2-20 of 1. That is where the bug record's title and public review threads point, and what the scans below show; I did not read the JDK's change itself.

I compared StrictMath.pow on JDK 25 and JDK 27 on a million inputs aimed at that branch: 500,262 have |x| within 2-20 of 1, and 389,513 of those reach that branch's arithmetic (a negative x with a non-integer y returns NaN first). The results differ on 1,258. On each of the 1,247 with a normal result, JDK 25 was at least 91 ulps off, and up to 653; JDK 27 is within 0.74 ulp on all of them.

So the old results broke StrictMath's own contract, which allows 1 ulp, and JDK 27 repaired them: a fix, not a regression.

It is a corner

Those 1,258 inputs were aimed at the branch. Elsewhere the change is hard to reach:

  • On 3,005,016 other inputs (vfdlibm's hard cases for pow, uniformly random bits, moderate magnitudes and values near 1), no result changed.
  • The 1,258 all have ||x| - 1| between about 2-23.5 and 2-21.6, and |y| between 231 and about 233, so their results are beyond 10±79.
  • With |x - 1| from 2-34 to 2-24 and |y| from 231 to 233 (results from about e±0.1 to e±500), 17 of 4,000,000 results changed, the query above among them, including some with results as large as those of the 1,258. There both JDKs are within 1 ulp; JDK 27's result is the nearer on 5 and JDK 25's on 12, too few to call either better. So the large errors came with ||x| - 1| near 2-22, not with the size of the result.
  • Per-second compounding, (1 + r/n)^(n·t) with n the seconds in a year, reaches the branch once n·t passes 231, about 68 years. Over rates from 0.01% to 20% and 70 to 300 years, none of its 462,000 results changed.

JDK 26 got the same fix in its update 26.0.2 (JDK-8385665, openjdk/jdk26u#210), but in Math.pow only: StrictMath.pow on JDK 26 still returns fdlibm 5.3's bits. On my x86-64 laptop even that Math.pow change is invisible by default, since the JIT uses its own pow there; with -XX:-UseLibmIntrinsic it shows, on the same 1,258 inputs and with JDK 27's bits (elsewhere that fallback returns StrictMath.pow's bits, but for the sign of a NaN result).

StrictMath moved once; Math moved three times

To see what else moves, I ran 20 double functions of StrictMath, the transcendental ones and their relatives with sqrt and IEEEremainder as controls, and Math's counterparts, on JDK 21, 25, 26 and 27, about 3 million inputs each.

StrictMath changed once in four JDKs, in one function, with a bug record and a Javadoc sentence. Math changed three times on this CPU, with no change to its Javadoc: cbrt and tanh from JDK 21 to 25, sinh from 25 to 26, each on 7% to 17% of the inputs. That is allowed, since Math only promises to be within an ulp or so, and the JIT picks its code per CPU; another CPU may move other functions.

So the contrast from the last post holds, with one correction. There I wrote that StrictMath gives "the same 64 bits on every JVM and every CPU". That is true within a JDK version, and across 21, 25 and 26 on everything I tried; from JDK 27, pow is the exception.

Rare per row, certain at scale

After the last post a reader wrote that "a single ulp can spiral into massive errors with big data". It does not spiral: rows do not feed each other in a SELECT or a SUM, and over a million rows the sums of exp(x) on Spark and on Photon differed by 3.6 parts in 1019. What does grow with the table is the number of rows where an ulp lands on a boundary and changes a decision.

To count them I took money arithmetic: amount * POWER(1 + rate, years) and amount * EXP(rate * years), with amounts up to a million in whole cents, rates in basis points up to 20% and 1 to 40 years, computed once with fdlibm (StrictMath's bits) and once with glibc 2.43, then rounded to cents.

rows amounts that differ cents that differ, POWER cents that differ, EXP
109 8.2% (POWER), 8.8% (EXP) 30 59
1010 8.2%, 8.8% 291 417

About one row in twelve (POWER) or eleven (EXP) gets a different amount, so a join, a GROUP BY or an equality test on these amounts sees two values for it. Different cents are far rarer, but ten times the rows gave seven to ten times as many. Two of them, checked end to end on Spark 4.3.0:

Spark (StrictMath) glibc's double, rounded by Spark
874,292.44 × exp(0.1342 × 39) 163,933,300.40 163,933,300.41
857,486.34 × power(1.1820, 40) 688,535,397.45 688,535,397.44

Two notes on that count. It is fdlibm against glibc, the difference between Spark and an engine that calls the C library, not JDK 25 against JDK 27: with at most 40 years, power(1 + rate, years) never reaches the branch JDK 27 changed. And it rounds as Spark's round does, from the double's shortest decimal: round(24471612.324999999D, 2) is 24471612.33 on Spark, though the double is below the half cent. Which rule another engine uses I have not measured.

What it means for vfdlibm

vfdlibm computes pow on whole vectors with the same bits as StrictMath. On the inputs above it still does, on JDK 25, 26 and 27: 0 lanes of 4,005,016 differ, at 256 and 512 bits. That is less clever than it sounds. vfdlibm computes in the vector only the lanes with |y| up to about 231, and hands each rarer lane to StrictMath.pow itself, so on the branch JDK 27 changed it returns whatever the running JDK returns.

It supports JDK 25 only, and stays so until a newer JDK has been checked function by function; issue #175 holds what is known. Where StrictMath departs from fdlibm, vfdlibm's rule is to follow StrictMath, as it already does for hypot's argument order; how one release would serve two JDKs whose StrictMath differs is the question #175 leaves open.

What I would do

  • Treat "the same bits as StrictMath" as "the same bits as StrictMath on this JDK". Record the JDK version next to results you compare by bits, such as golden files, checksums or hashes.
  • Expect a JDK upgrade to move Math's results, and now and then StrictMath's, and read the release notes for both.
  • Do not join, group or deduplicate on a computed double you do not control, and round once, at the end.

How I checked

Everything is in docs/blog/strictmath-not-frozen, with each data file's provenance at its top; the two lists of changed inputs, pow-strictmath-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt and pow-moderate-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt, are described in the files that made them. The JDKs were Ubuntu 26.04's 21.0.13.1, 25.0.5.1 and 26.0.2, and 27.0.1.1 from jdk.java.net, on an AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370; vfdlibm was 0.3.0 from Maven Central; Spark was the official 4.3.0 build, checked against Apache's SHA-512.

  • JdkScan.java and compare.py: vfdlibm's 13 functions on JDK 25, 26 and 27, with vfdlibm checked against each (jdk-scan-2026-11-25.txt); the 1,258 inputs with both results are in pow-strictmath-jdk25-vs-jdk27.txt.
  • StrictScan.java: those 20 functions of StrictMath and Math on JDK 21 to 27 (strict-scan-2026-11-25.txt).
  • errors.py, Moderate.java, Compound.java and power-jdk.sql: the errors against an 80-digit pow, the ordinary sizes, the compounding and the Spark query (pow-jdk27-detail-2026-11-25.txt).
  • flips.c, FlipsCheck.java and round-check.sql: the cents, with fdlibm checked against StrictMath first (cents-fdlibm-vs-glibc-2026-11-25.txt).

The bug records' facts are from their public pull requests and the two Javadoc pages; no JDK source was read for this post.
